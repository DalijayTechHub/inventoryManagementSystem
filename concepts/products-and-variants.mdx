Every item you sell, stock, or track in Blinks Inventory exists within a three-level hierarchy: a **product** at the top, one or more **variants** beneath it, and a unique **SKU** that identifies each variant for tracking purposes. This structure is flexible enough to handle a simple catalog with a handful of items as well as a complex assortment with thousands of combinations of sizes, colors, and configurations — all without duplicating data or losing traceability. Understanding how these three levels relate to each other is the first step to building a well-organized catalog in Blinks.

## The Product Hierarchy

| Level | What It Represents | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Product** | The top-level item — a general thing you sell or stock | `Classic T-Shirt` |
| **Variant** | A specific version of that product defined by one or more attributes | `Classic T-Shirt / Large / Blue` |
| **SKU** | A unique identifier assigned to a specific variant, used for all tracking | `TSHIRT-LG-BLU` |

Every product must have at least one variant, and every variant must have a SKU. If your product has no meaningful variants — for example, a single-size printed poster — you still create one variant (often called `Default`) with its own SKU.

## Products

A product is the parent container for a group of related variants. It holds information that is shared across all its variants:

- **Product name** — the display name used in the dashboard, reports, and order documents (for example, `Classic T-Shirt`).
- **Description** — a free-text description for internal reference or use in integrations.
- **Category** — assigns the product to a category (for example, `Apparel > Tops`). Categories are used to filter the catalog, generate category-level reports, and group products in integrations.
- **Tags** — free-form labels you can add to a product to support custom filtering (for example, `summer-2024`, `clearance`, `bundle-eligible`).

Products themselves do not have stock levels. Stock is always tracked at the variant (SKU) level.

## Variants

A variant represents one specific, purchasable or stockable version of a product. Each variant is defined by one or more **attributes** — characteristics that distinguish it from other variants of the same product.

Common attribute types include:

- **Size** (for example, XS, S, M, L, XL, or numeric sizing)
- **Color** (for example, Blue, Red, Natural)
- **Material** (for example, Cotton, Polyester)
- **Configuration** (for example, 500ml, 1L, 5L for a liquid product)

You define attribute names and values yourself in Blinks — the system does not enforce a fixed set of attribute types, so you can model any product type.

<Note>
  Each combination of attribute values creates a distinct variant. A T-Shirt with 4 sizes and 5 colors has up to 20 variants, each with its own SKU and its own independent stock level.
</Note>

## SKUs (Stock Keeping Units)

A SKU is the unique identifier that Blinks uses to track inventory at the most granular level. Every stock movement, purchase order line, sales order line, and inventory record references a SKU — not a product name.

SKUs must be **unique across your entire catalog**. You define them yourself, but a consistent naming convention makes your catalog much easier to manage. Common patterns include:

- `[CATEGORY]-[SIZE]-[COLOR]` → `TSHIRT-LG-BLU`
- `[SUPPLIER_CODE]-[INTERNAL_ID]` → `ACM-00427`
- `[PRODUCT_SHORT]-[VARIANT_HASH]` → `POLO-4XBL`

<Tip>
  Choose your SKU format before you start importing or creating products. Changing SKUs after they appear on orders, movement records, and integrations is disruptive and requires careful coordination.
</Tip>

## Barcode and UPC Fields

Each variant supports two scan codes alongside the SKU:

- **Barcode** — an arbitrary scan code you define, often matching the code physically printed on the item or its packaging.
- **UPC (Universal Product Code)** — the 12-digit industry-standard code, typically used for retail or wholesale products sold through third-party channels.

Both fields are optional but recommended if your team scans items during receiving, picking, or cycle counts. Blinks lets you look up a variant by scanning either its SKU barcode, its custom barcode, or its UPC.

## Cost Price and Selling Price

Each variant stores two price fields:

- **Cost price** — what you pay to procure or produce one unit of the variant. Used in inventory valuation, margin calculations, and purchase order prefilling.
- **Selling price** — the default price at which you sell one unit. Used on sales orders as the prefilled unit price (which you can override per order line).

Neither price field affects stock tracking directly, but accurate cost prices are essential for meaningful inventory valuation reports and for understanding your gross margin per SKU.

## Product Categories and Tags

**Categories** form a structured hierarchy that you define under **Settings > Categories**. You might set up a hierarchy like `Apparel > Tops > T-Shirts` or `Food & Beverage > Oils > Olive Oil`. Categories power:

- Catalog browsing and filtering in the dashboard
- Category-level inventory and sales reports
- Grouping in integrations that sync your catalog to an e-commerce platform

**Tags** are free-form and unstructured. Add as many tags as you like to a product to support ad-hoc filtering, bulk actions, or custom logic in your integrations. Unlike categories, tags do not form a hierarchy and a product can have any number of them.

<Info>
  Tags and categories serve different purposes. Use categories for your permanent, structured taxonomy and tags for temporary labels, campaigns, or contextual groupings that change over time.
</Info>
